How to host a startup circle that founders actually come back to

Big startup events are good for business cards and bad for honest conversation. A startup circle is the opposite: eight to twenty founders in a room, meeting regularly, talking about what's actually going wrong.

A format that works

  1. Check-in (15 minutes). Everyone gives one sentence each: what they're building and how they're feeling about it this week.
  2. Hot seats (60 to 75 minutes). Two or three founders each bring one real problem, such as pricing, a co-founder conflict or a stalled launch. They get five minutes to explain it, then the group asks questions before giving advice.
  3. Quick asks (15 minutes). Anyone can ask for an intro, a tester or a tool recommendation.
  4. Close (10 minutes). Each person names one thing they'll do before the next circle.

Rules worth stating out loud

  • What's shared in the circle stays there.
  • Questions before advice.
  • No pitching. Everyone in the room is a peer.

Getting the room right

Set the tables in a U so everyone can see everyone, and put a whiteboard or flip chart at the open end. Keep it cool, because a stuffy room kills a two-hour conversation. Make sure there are plug points for laptops, and leave a corner free for the inevitable one-on-one chats afterwards.

Meeting monthly in the same place helps more than you'd expect. When people know where to go, the circle starts to feel like theirs.
