What is a grief table, and how do you host one?

Most of us don't have anywhere to talk about grief. Friends change the subject, workplaces give you three days, and therapy isn't for everyone. A grief table is a simple fix: a small group of people who've lost someone, sitting around one table, with time set aside to talk and listen.

It isn't therapy and it isn't a support group with a programme. It's closer to a long dinner where everyone knows why they're there.

What happens at a grief table

A host welcomes people, explains how the evening works, and often starts with a gentle prompt, such as "Tell us one thing about the person you're missing." People take turns. Nobody has to speak. There's tea, maybe something to eat, and plenty of quiet.

Two hours is usually right. Shorter feels rushed and longer gets tiring.

How to host one

  1. Keep it small. Six to twelve people lets everyone speak without anyone feeling watched.
  2. Set two or three ground rules. What's said stays in the room. Listen without fixing. Passing is always fine.
  3. Choose one long table. Sitting side by side and across from each other feels less exposed than a circle of chairs.
  4. Mind the light. Bright overhead light makes people feel on show. Lamps and softer corners help.
  5. Plan the ending. Close with a small ritual, such as saying a name aloud or lighting a candle, so people leave with a sense of completion.
  6. Share help. Have details of local counselling and helplines ready for anyone who needs more support.

Why the room matters

A café is too public and a living room is too personal. A grief table works best somewhere neutral and quiet that you can close the door on.
